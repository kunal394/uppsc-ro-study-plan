Study, recall, mocks and repair

Learn the whole topic, not only its questions

  • Preview the subtopic checklist and read the original explanation. Write two questions you expect the source to answer.
  • Read the named chapter/sections selectively. Explain cause–mechanism–effect, comparisons and exceptions; label maps/tables with dates and units.
  • Close the book and perform the topic's learning checks. Produce the day's map/table/summary. If you cannot explain it, reread the smallest missing section.
  • Attempt the matching hidden-answer cards only afterwards. They are a selected adaptation bank, not full syllabus coverage. Supplement unseen practice from one lawful source if needed.
  • Review why you were wrong, then revisit after 1, 7 and 21 days. A repeated failure triggers conceptual repair, not another hundred unreviewed questions.

Time and source discipline

Ordinary 4-hour day: 90 GS + 45 Hindi + 45 PYQ/explanation + 30 CA/UP + 30 recall minutes. Two-hour version: 40 + 25 + 25 + 15 + 15. Reduce volume and extend the cycle; do not remove explanations. Choose one reference per subject. A missed day is resumed, not doubled.

Mock versus drill

Weeks 5–8 reserve one full-paper session/week, weeks 9–12 two. A current-pattern mock needs 200 unseen questions and a continuous 180-minute attempt; an annotated old paper or the short embedded bank is not one. Historical retests measure retrieval/pacing, not unseen readiness. At 2 hours/day, split over two days and label it a split attempt.

Error-log structure

In a private notebook record exact file/Q, topic, answer/confidence (known/reasoned/guessed/blank), cause (concept/memory/reading/guess/time), correct rule and source, unresolved status, and +1/+7/+21 review dates. Verify against an official final key if obtained or a trustworthy independent source; never take handwriting as evidence.

Practice score calculator

Current 200-mark arithmetic: correct − wrong/3. Blanks receive no penalty; exclude unresolved answers and label partial scores. Not a cutoff prediction.

After Day 84

If the exam is distant, keep one unseen paper/week, rolling current affairs and a weak-topic cycle. If foundations are weak, extend their first-pass work. If the exam is close, use the official date to taper new reading and prioritise recall/rest. After prelims, move to the descriptive roadmap.
