Amendments, basic structure and schedules

Expanded editorial learning guide

Topic and subtopic breakdown

  • Ordinary-law changes versus Article 368
  • Special majority
  • State ratification where required
  • Basic-structure limitation
  • Schedules by subject
  • Constitutional change versus ordinary legislation

Understand before memorising

Do not use 'majority' as a single undifferentiated rule. Different constitutional changes follow different procedures; some Article 368 amendments also require ratification by at least half the states.

Basic structure limits the amending power: Parliament may amend the Constitution, but cannot use that power to destroy its basic structure. Schedules organise subjects such as lists and languages; learn their purposes before attempting all entries.

Original worked example (not a PYQ):

Make a decision tree: is the proposal ordinary legislation, a constitutional amendment, or an amendment requiring state ratification? Identify the procedure before discussing the proposal's substance.
