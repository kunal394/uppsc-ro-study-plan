Fundamental Rights

Expanded editorial learning guide

Topic and subtopic breakdown

  • State and law: Articles 12–13
  • Equality: Articles 14–18
  • Freedoms and safeguards: Articles 19–22
  • Exploitation: Articles 23–24
  • Religion: Articles 25–28
  • Cultural/educational rights: Articles 29–30
  • Remedies: Article 32
  • Citizens versus all persons

Understand before memorising

Read each right as a relationship: who holds it, against whom it operates, and what qualifications apply. Rights are not all identical in scope: Article 19 concerns citizens, while Article 21 protects persons.

Article 15 addresses discrimination in its specified contexts; Article 16 concerns public employment. A question can use similar grounds but a different legal setting. Always identify the setting before selecting an article.

Original worked example (not a PYQ):

A restriction on government-job eligibility and a restriction on access to a public place should not automatically be assigned the same article. Build a setting → right → article table, then check the actual provision.

Closed-book learning objectives

  • Explain Articles 15 and 16 separately
  • Identify which examples concern citizens and which concern persons
